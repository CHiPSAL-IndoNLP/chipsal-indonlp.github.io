Overview & Workshop Focus

South Asia is home to hundreds of languages spoken by nearly two billion people across multiple major language families—most notably Indo-Aryan and Dravidian, alongside Tibeto-Burman, Austroasiatic, and others. Despite this immense linguistic diversity and rich cultural heritage, South Asian languages remain significantly under-resourced and under-represented in modern Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Model (LLM) research.

For COLING 2027, CHiPSAL and IndoNLP are joining forces to host a unified two-day forum bringing together researchers, linguists, and technologists to address shared bottlenecks, build open digital infrastructure, and foster cross-family comparative research.

CHiPSAL Focus

Challenges in Processing South Asian Languages

CHiPSAL emphasizes foundational linguistic, script-level, and structural challenges across spoken, written, signed, and multimodal South Asian languages. It addresses core technical bottlenecks including Unicode encoding and orthographic complexities, rich morphology, dialectal variation, standardization, pervasive code-mixing, digital heritage preservation, and systematic model benchmarking.

IndoNLP Focus

NLP for Indo-Aryan and Dravidian Languages

IndoNLP focuses on bridging the technological and resource gap specifically for Indo-Aryan and Dravidian Languages (IADL). It highlights the creation and annotation of cleaned digital corpora, LLM adaptation for complex IADL syntax and semantics, speech and OCR technologies, cross-lingual systems, and downstream applications such as sentiment analysis, summarization, hate speech detection, and combating misinformation, fake news, and spam.

Alignment with COLING 2027 Special Theme (“NLP for Linguistics”): In synergy with COLING 2027’s special theme, this joint workshop encourages research that not only builds practical NLP tools for South Asian languages, but also leverages computational modeling to deepen scientific understanding of South Asian linguistic typology, dialectal variation, and underrepresented speech communities.

Themes & Topics of Interest

We invite substantial, original, and unpublished research papers (long and short), resource/dataset papers, and system demonstrations covering spoken, written, signed, and multimodal South Asian languages (with particular attention to Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, and regional South Asian varieties). Topics include, but are not limited to:

1. Scripts, Orthography & Core Linguistic Modeling

  • Encoding and Unicode issues in South Asian scripts
  • Orthographic complexities and their impact on language technology
  • Morphological analysis, generation, syntax, and semantic modeling in South Asian languages
  • Dialectal variations, language identification, dialect detection, and language standardisation
  • Code-mixing, code-switching, and multilingualism in South Asian contexts

2. Language Resources, Benchmarks & Heritage Preservation

  • Developing cleaned Indo-Aryan and Dravidian language corpora (Unicode) and digital linguistic resources
  • Building annotated datasets for spoken, written, signed, and multimodal South Asian languages
  • Benchmarking and evaluating NLP models across South Asian languages and dialects
  • Preserving linguistic and cultural heritage through language technology

3. Large Language Models, Translation & Speech/Vision Systems

  • Large Language Models (LLMs) for South Asian, Indo-Aryan, and Dravidian languages (pre-training, instruction tuning, alignment, and efficiency)
  • Machine translation and cross-lingual transfer systems
  • Speech technologies: Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Text-to-Speech (TTS) synthesis
  • Optical Character Recognition (OCR) systems, information extraction, and knowledge modelling

4. Downstream Applications & Online Safety

  • Sentiment analysis, opinion mining, and text summarisation
  • Hate speech, abusive language, and offensive content detection
  • Detection of fake news, rumors, spam, phishing, conspiracy theories, and misinformation
  • Real-world NLP applications in education, healthcare, governance, and accessibility for South Asian communities

Submission Guidelines (COLING 2027 Standards)

Deadline: January 15, 2027

Track 1: Direct Workshop Submissions

Intended for original, unpublished research papers submitted directly to the joint workshop. All direct submissions undergo rigorous double-blind peer review by 3 reviewers with relevant domain expertise from the joint Program Committee.

Deadline: February 15, 2027

Track 2: COLING 2027 ARR Submissions

Intended for papers previously submitted to the COLING 2027 ACL Rolling Review (ARR) cycle (following COLING 2027 main conference notifications on February 10, 2027). Authors must submit their manuscript along with their ARR reviews, meta-review, and a brief response/revision summary detailing how reviewer comments have been or will be addressed.

Paper Categories, Formatting & Page Limits

  • Templates & Language: All submissions must be written in English and formatted using the official *ACL / COLING 2027 style templates (LaTeX, Overleaf, or Word).
  • Long Papers: Up to 8 pages of main content.
  • Short Papers & System Demonstrations: Up to 4 pages of main content.
  • Unlimited References & Appendices: Page limits apply strictly to the main body of the paper. Papers may include unlimited pages for references, technical appendices, data/code statements, and supplementary materials (such as source code, datasets, or multimedia files).
  • Camera-Ready Page Allowance: Upon acceptance, both long and short papers are granted 1 additional page of main content (up to 9 pages for long papers and 5 pages for short papers) to incorporate reviewer feedback.

Mandatory Sections & Reproducibility

  • Mandatory Limitations Section: Following COLING 2027 requirements, all submissions must include a dedicated Limitations section at the end of the paper (after the Conclusion and before the References; excluded from the page limit).
  • Ethics & Data/Code Availability: Authors are strongly encouraged to include an Ethics Statement (excluded from the page limit) and a Data/Code Availability Statement. Newly created language resources are encouraged to be documented via open repositories or the LRE Map.

Anonymity, Preprints & Publication Policy

  • Double-Blind Anonymization: All direct submissions (including appendices and linked repositories) must be fully anonymized for double-blind review. Remove all author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and self-identifying repository URLs from the initial submission.
  • Preprint & Multiple Submission Policy: Consistent with COLING 2027 and ACL policies, there is no anonymity period restricting arXiv or preprint posting. Concurrent submissions to other venues must be declared at submission time; if accepted to the joint CHiPSAL–IndoNLP workshop, the paper must be withdrawn from other archival venues.
  • Presentation & Proceedings: Accepted papers will be presented as either Oral or Poster / Poster+Demo presentations (with no distinction in the proceedings between oral and poster papers) and will be published in the official COLING 2027 Joint Workshop Proceedings in the ACL Anthology. At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the workshop and present the work.
