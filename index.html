Workshop Description

The workshop brings together the Challenges in Processing South Asian Languages (CHiPSAL) and Workshop on Natural Language Processing for Indo-Aryan and Dravidian Languages (IndoNLP) communities to advance research and collaboration in computational linguistics and natural language processing. It provides a unified forum for researchers, linguists, and technologists to discuss language resources, language technologies, and the challenges of supporting diverse and under-resourced languages across South Asia.

The rapid advancement of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Models (LLMs) has transformed the landscape of computational linguistics. However, Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, and other South Asian languages—which represent a significant portion of South Asia’s linguistic heritage spoken by nearly two billion people—remain under-resourced and under-researched in these technological developments. This joint workshop aims to bridge this gap by bringing together researchers, linguists, and technologists to focus on unique linguistic and computational challenges, from Unicode encoding, orthography, rich morphology, dialectal variation, and code-mixing to multimodal, signed, and spoken language processing.

Participants will explore innovative methods for creating and annotating digital corpora, adapt LLMs to handle complex syntax and semantics, develop speech and OCR technologies, and build impactful downstream applications. Furthermore, the workshop provides a platform for sharing best practices, open tools, and evaluation benchmarks, strengthening the digital infrastructure necessary for linguistic diversity and cultural preservation in the digital age.
